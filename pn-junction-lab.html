Intrinsic Semiconductor

Pure silicon or germanium behaves as a semiconductor. At room temperature, a small number of covalent bonds break and create free electrons and holes.

Electron Hole

Key Idea

Electron–hole pair generation: thermal energy can free a valence electron. The missing electron in the bond behaves like a positively charged hole.

N-Type Summary

Dopant: Pentavalent donor

Examples: P, As, Sb

Majority carriers: Electrons

Minority carriers: Holes

Ionized donor: Fixed positive ion

Conventional current direction is opposite to electron drift direction.

P-Type Semiconductor Animation

A trivalent acceptor atom such as boron forms only three complete covalent bonds, producing a vacancy that behaves as a hole.

55%

P-Type Summary

Dopant: Trivalent acceptor

Examples: B, Al, Ga

Majority carriers: Holes

Minority carriers: Electrons

Ionized acceptor: Fixed negative ion

Hole motion represents successive movement of valence electrons into nearby vacancies.

Formation of a PN Junction

Diffusion

What Happens?

1. Electrons diffuse from N to P.

2. Holes diffuse from P to N.

3. Recombination leaves fixed ions near the junction.

4. A depletion region is created.

5. The built-in electric field opposes further diffusion.

The depletion region contains very few mobile carriers.

Forward Bias

0.60 V

Barrier is reduced; majority carriers cross the junction more easily.

Reverse Bias

5.0 V

Barrier height and depletion width increase. Only a very small reverse saturation current flows before breakdown.

Suggested real-lab components: PN diode (e.g. 1N400x or 1N4148), regulated DC supply, 1 kΩ series resistor, milliammeter / digital multimeter, voltmeter, breadboard and patch cords.

Experiment Procedure

  1. Connect the diode in forward bias: P-side/anode to positive supply and N-side/cathode to negative through a current-limiting resistor.
  2. Increase supply gradually and record diode voltage and current.
  3. Plot forward current versus diode voltage and identify the knee region.
  4. Reverse the diode polarity for reverse bias.
  5. Increase reverse voltage carefully and record the small reverse current. Do not exceed the device rating in a real laboratory.
  6. Plot reverse current versus reverse voltage and compare both regions.
